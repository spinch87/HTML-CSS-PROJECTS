Introduction

Cats are small domesticated mammals known for their independence, curiosity, agility, and distinctive personalities.

Domestic cats have lived alongside humans for thousands of years and are now one of the most popular companion animals in the world.

A cat's scientific classification begins with Felis catus, the species name for the domestic cat.

  • Cats are mammals.
  • Cats are carnivores.
  • Most domestic cats have retractable claws.
Cat Behavior

Cats communicate and interact with their surroundings through a combination of body language, sounds, scent, and physical behavior.

A cat may knead with its paws when it feels comfortable or relaxed. This behavior begins when kittens knead their mother's belly while nursing.

Cats are naturally curious animals and often investigate new objects, people, and environments.

A cat's pupils can change size depending on lighting and emotional state. A relaxed cat may also slowly blink at a person.

  • Kneading can indicate comfort.
  • Tail position can provide clues about mood.
  • Scratching is a natural cat behavior.
  • Cats often sleep for many hours each day.
Cat Communication

Cats use many different sounds to communicate. These can include meowing, purring, hissing, growling, and chirping.

The familiar meow is commonly used by domestic cats when interacting with humans.

Purring is often associated with contentment, although cats may also purr in other situations, including when they are stressed or uncomfortable.

Body language is another important part of feline communication. Flattened ears and an arched back can indicate that a cat feels threatened.

  • Meowing is commonly directed toward humans.
  • Purring can occur when a cat is relaxed.
  • Hissing can signal fear or discomfort.
  • A raised tail can indicate a friendly mood.
Cat Nutrition

Cats are obligate carnivores, meaning their natural diet requires nutrients that are primarily obtained from animal-based foods.

A complete commercial cat food should provide essential nutrients such as protein, fats, vitamins, minerals, and water.

Fresh drinking water should always be available. Some cats prefer moving water, which is why water fountains can be appealing.

Foods intended for humans should not automatically be considered safe for cats. Some foods can be harmful or toxic to them.

A cat's diet can be represented conceptually as protein + fat + vitamins + minerals + water.

  • Provide fresh water every day.
  • Choose nutritionally complete cat food.
  • Avoid feeding cats potentially toxic foods.
  • Monitor portions to maintain a healthy weight.
Cat Care

Responsible cat care includes providing appropriate food, clean water, a safe home, enrichment, grooming, and regular veterinary attention.

Cats benefit from having clean litter boxes placed in quiet and accessible locations.

Scratching posts and other enrichment items allow cats to perform natural behaviors while protecting household furniture.

Regular grooming can help remove loose fur and provides an opportunity to notice changes in a cat's skin, coat, or body.

A simple daily care routine might include feed(), refreshWater(), and cleanLitter().

  • Keep litter boxes clean.
  • Provide appropriate scratching surfaces.
  • Give cats opportunities for play and exercise.
  • Schedule regular veterinary checkups.
  • Keep dangerous household substances away from cats.
Fun Facts

Cats have excellent hearing and can detect sounds at frequencies that humans cannot hear.

A cat's whiskers are highly sensitive and help it gather information about nearby objects and its environment.

Cats spend a significant portion of their lives sleeping. Their sleeping habits help conserve energy for periods of activity.

Many cats enjoy high places because elevated positions can give them a better view of their surroundings.

One fun way to describe a cat's daily routine is eat(); sleep(); play(); repeat();

  • Cats have excellent night vision.
  • Many cats enjoy climbing.
  • Every cat has its own personality.
